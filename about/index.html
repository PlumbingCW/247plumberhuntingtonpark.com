What we do and who we serve

We serve homeowners, renters, landlords and small businesses in Huntington Park and the towns around it. Our work runs from drain cleaning and leak detection to water heater replacement and repiping.

Most of our neighbors rent, and many live in older buildings with shared lines. That shapes how we work. We explain findings in plain language and write down what we did. Landlords and tenants both get the same facts.

How to check any plumber before you hire

This applies to us and to every other company on your list. It takes five minutes.

  1. 1
    Ask for the license numberCalifornia plumbing contractors hold a C-36 license from the Contractors State License Board.
  2. 2
    Look it up yourselfUse the CSLB license check online and confirm the name, status and classification match.
  3. 3
    Confirm insuranceAsk for proof of liability coverage and workers compensation before anyone enters your home.
  4. 4
    Get the scope and price in writingA clear written price protects both sides if the job changes.

What local plumbing looks like here

Huntington Park incorporated in 1906 as a streetcar suburb, and many homes and small commercial buildings are decades old. Older supply lines and cast iron drains are common, which is why we recommend a camera or pressure test before anyone cuts into a wall.

About 73 percent of occupied homes in the city are rented. If you are a tenant, call us for an emergency, then loop in your landlord. If you are an owner, ask us to write up what we found so you can share it with a tenant or property manager.

6

What we will not do

We will not quote a price before we know what is wrong, which is why the first step is always a diagnosis, promise an arrival time we cannot control, or sell a replacement when a repair will hold. We will not name a license number on this page, because the right way to verify us is the state lookup above.

If a job needs a specialist, such as a gas utility or an electrician, we tell you and wait for them to finish before we continue. Safety comes before schedule on every call, and that includes ours.

7

Questions to ask any plumber

Ask whether the price includes cleanup and parts, whether a camera or pressure test comes first, and who is on the hook if the job grows. A company that answers these plainly is worth a call.

Then compare the answers. Cheap and vague usually costs more than clear and fair, especially when a wall is already open and you are out of options.

8

What to keep after the job

Ask for a written summary of what was found, what was done and what parts went in. Keep it with your water heater paperwork or lease file, because it answers most questions a landlord, buyer or insurer asks later.

If we recommend follow-up work, such as a camera look at a sewer line or a tank flush next year, we will say when and why. You decide, and nothing is added without your approval.
