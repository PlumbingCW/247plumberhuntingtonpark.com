What the city's housing numbers mean for a repair call

Verified local fact: the 2020 census counts 15,494 housing units in Huntington Park, and about 73 percent of occupied homes are rented. The city's own housing element describes it as one of the densest cities in the country and nearly entirely built out.

For a plumbing call, the takeaway is who decides. A tenant can stop the damage and report it, but a repair inside the walls or under the slab usually needs the owner's approval. If you manage the building, call us directly and we will price the work before we start.

Tenant or owner: who calls first

Tenants should shut off the water at the fixture or the main if it is safe, take photos, and tell the landlord in writing. Owners and managers can book the repair. Either way, our line is the same and the price is explained before work begins.

Hard water: check who supplies your address

The City of Huntington Park 2025 Consumer Confidence Report gives total hardness of about 214 mg/L (roughly 12.5 grains per gallon) for local groundwater and about 235 mg/L for imported Metropolitan Water District supply. The US Geological Survey calls anything above 180 mg/L very hard.

The city water system lists about 6,600 service connections, which is fewer than the city's housing units. We cannot tell you which supplier serves your address, so check your water bill. If you are on city supply, a water softener and regular water heater service are worth pricing.
