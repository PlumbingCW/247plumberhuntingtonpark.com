A burst or stuck supply valve is an emergency

The worst faucet calls are not drips. They are a stop valve that snaps off in your hand, a supply line that bursts under a sink, or a handle that spins freely while water keeps pouring. These cannot wait for morning because the water keeps running until someone closes a valve upstream.

If the valve under the sink will not close, go to the main. In Huntington Park, where many homes are rented apartments and duplexes, the main may be outside or in a shared meter area, so ask the manager now rather than during a flood.

A faucet that is more than about 15 years old, pitted, corroded at the base or built from cheap pot metal rarely returns a good result from parts. Replacement costs more now and less across the next decade.

5

Broken stop valves and supply lines under the sink

The two small valves under a sink or behind a toilet are the first line of defense, and they fail more often than the faucet. Mineral scale locks the stem, and the packing nut leaks when turned. Replacing a stop is a small job when the pipe behind it is sound, and a bigger one when the pipe is corroded.

Flexible braided supply lines should be replaced when you swap a faucet. Old plastic or thin rubber lines can burst, and a burst line is the leak we are called for most at night. Related fixes for toilet stops are covered under toilet repair and installation.

When the pipe behind the valve is the problem

If the stub-out is green, flaking or loose in the wall, a new valve will not hold. We shut off at the main, cut back to sound pipe and re-solder or use push-fit fittings. A leak inside the wall is a different job, and a burst pipe repair visit covers it.

6

Shower valves, showerheads and the tub spout

A showerhead that drips after you shut off the water usually has a worn washer, a bad cartridge in the valve or sediment under the seat. Replacing the head and the Teflon tape at the arm often fixes a leak where the arm meets the wall. A handle that will not hold temperature or will not shut off all the way points to the cartridge.

A tub spout that gushes while the shower is on has a failing diverter. If the wall is wet or stained under the tub, the leak is behind the tile, and a leak detection visit with moisture meters and thermal imaging can find it without opening more wall than needed.

8

Shower and tub drains that will not clear

Standing water at your ankles is nearly always hair and soap collected on the strainer, stopper or just below it. A hand drain snake or a small drum cable pulls it up, and chemical openers often leave the same clog and corrode the trap arm. If the tub, a sink and a toilet all drain slowly together, the blockage is farther down and drain cleaning with a larger machine is the fix.

10

Hard water, scale and weak flow

Huntington Park's supply is very hard by the USGS scale. The city's 2025 water quality report gives an average of 214 mg/L for groundwater and 235 mg/L for imported water, so scale clogs aerators, locks cartridges and chalks up shower holes. Soak an aerator in vinegar for an hour before replacing it. If scale returns every few months, test your tap and consider filtration and softening.

11

Kitchen sinks: sprayers, pull-downs and what sits underneath

Kitchen sinks hide more than bathroom ones. A pull-down hose can crack and drip into the cabinet, a side sprayer diverter can fail, and a garbage disposal or dishwasher connection can mimic a faucet leak. Run the water for a few minutes with a dry paper towel under each fitting. The wet spot tells you whether it is the faucet, a supply line or the drain basket. Disposal leaks are covered under garbage disposal repair.
