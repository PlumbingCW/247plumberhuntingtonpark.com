2

When a backed-up drain is an emergency

A slow sink can wait for a daytime visit. A drain that is overflowing, a toilet that will not stop rising, or sewage coming up in a tub cannot, because the water has nowhere to go but onto your floor.

Call our emergency line right away if wastewater is reaching flooring or drywall, if every drain in the home is slow at the same time, or if there is a sewage smell with standing water. After-hours drain calls get the same cable machines and the same clear price before work starts as a Tuesday morning visit.

In a dense, built-out city like Huntington Park, many homes and apartments share lines, so one stoppage can show up in a neighbor's unit. If you rent, tell your landlord or property manager the moment water rises, and send us the address so we can coordinate access.

4

Snaking and cabling: how the blockage actually comes out

People use the words snake, auger and cable interchangeably, but the tool changes with the pipe. Picking the wrong one either misses the clog or scratches the pipe.

Hand augers for traps and short runs

A hand-cranked auger reaches about 25 feet and is the right choice for a toilet trap or a single sink. It spins through the blockage and pulls it back, which protects fixtures from the heavy force a bigger machine can apply.

Drum machines and sectional cables

For lines of 2 inches or more we run a motorized drum machine or sectional cable with a cutting head. The head bores through a grease plug or root hair, and then a second pass with a scraper blade cleans the pipe wall so the opening does not close again in a week.

When the cable is not enough

Cabling opens a channel, but it leaves a coating of grease on the walls. If a line keeps clogging after being snaked, the better answer is often hydro jetting, which scours the full pipe diameter with high-pressure water.

5

Kitchen sink clog: grease, rice and the dishwasher hose

The kitchen branch is the most common drain call we hear. Cooking fat goes down hot and liquid, then cools into a waxy lining that catches coffee grounds, rice and eggshell, and the opening narrows over months.

Signs you have a grease clog rather than a stuck object: the sink drains slowly for weeks, the water smells sour, and a plunger moves the water but never clears it. A double-basin sink that fills the second side when you run the first is a classic sign.

We cable the line past the trap arm, then check the dishwasher air gap and drain hose, because a kinked or clogged hose makes the dishwasher spill into the sink. If the garbage disposal is jammed or leaking at the same time, our garbage disposal repair team can handle both in one visit.

6

Slow shower and tub drains

Hair, soap scum and body oil form a rope-like mass just below the strainer or in the trap, and it rarely sits more than a few feet down. A short cable or barbed plastic strip often pulls it out in one pass.

What affects the cost of drain cleaning

Drain cleaning prices vary more than most plumbing work because the job can be a ten-minute trap clear or a two-hour main line run. These are the factors that move the number.

Which line is blockedA single fixture trap is the simplest. A branch line or the main line needs bigger equipment and more cable length.
Access to a cleanoutA working cleanout lets us cable straight in. Without one we may need to pull a toilet or work through a roof vent.
Cause of the clogGrease, roots and a collapsed pipe each require different tools, and some need a camera before and after.
Time of the callA midnight emergency takes a technician off call rotation, so after-hours work can carry a different rate than a scheduled visit.
Homeowners insurance rarely pays for a clog itself. It may cover resulting water damage, and some policies offer a separate sewer backup endorsement. Check your own policy and ask for the technician's notes so you have a record.

Try this first, and what to leave alone

A few safe moves can save a visit. Others make a plumber's job harder or put you at risk.

  • ✓
    Use a flange plungerFor toilets, use the type with a rubber flap. For sinks, block the overflow opening with a wet rag so the pressure goes down the pipe.
  • ✓
    Try hot tap water, not boilingVery hot water softens grease. Boiling water can crack old porcelain and soften PVC joints.
  • ✓
    Skip chemical drain openersThey sit in standing water, can burn skin and eyes, and leave a hazard for the plumber. Tell us if you already poured some in.
  • ✓
    Do not use a rented power auger on old pipeCast iron and clay lines can be cracked by a machine run too hard. A cable that is too aggressive turns a clog into a repair.

If the plunger did nothing in a few minutes of honest effort, stop and call. Repeated pressure on a fully blocked line only pushes water back up.

9

When the drain pipe itself is leaking

Sometimes the clog is a symptom and the real problem is a damaged drain line. Signs include a damp cabinet floor that stays wet with the supply off, a stain on a ceiling below a bathroom, or a musty smell with no visible source.

We run a camera through the line when the cable keeps hitting a stop at the same distance. If the footage shows a cracked, offset or collapsed section, the right next step is a sewer camera inspection report and a repair quote, not another round of snaking.

10

Shared stacks, renters and apartment buildings

About 73 percent of occupied homes in Huntington Park are rented, so many drain calls involve more than one household. A kitchen stack shared by units can clog at the lowest unit even though the blockage began upstairs.

Landlords and tenants are both welcome to call. Tell us who authorized the visit, send the unit number, and we will explain in plain words what the blockage was so the right party handles the bill.
