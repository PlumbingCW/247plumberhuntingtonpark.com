Seven in ten Bell homes are rented, and that shapes every call

With about 72 percent of homes rented, the person who notices the leak is often not the person who owns the pipe. Tenants shut off water and call the landlord, managers authorize repairs, and a plumber ends up working between them. We are comfortable with that three-way call and can give a manager a written summary of what we found.

Density adds a second wrinkle. In a duplex, fourplex or apartment block, drains from several kitchens and bathrooms often share one vertical stack and one lateral to the street. A blockage low in that line can push water up through the lowest fixture, usually the ground-floor tub or floor drain.

Check which company bills your Bell address

Bell is not served by one water system. Golden State Water Company, which keeps a 24-hour line, serves part of the area, and small mutual companies such as Tract 180 Water Company, with an office on Florence Avenue, serve other blocks. Look at your bill before you call anyone about pressure or a main.

If your bill comes from Golden State Water, its 2025 report for the Bell and Bell Gardens system shows total hardness averaging about 210 mg/L, roughly 12 grains per gallon, from groundwater blended with imported supply. Numbers shift by block and season, so test your tap, and ask your own provider for its annual report.

When a Bell backup is a building problem, not a unit problem

If a toilet backs up and a downstairs neighbor reports the same, stop all water use in the building and call right away. A camera run through the cleanout shows whether roots, grease or a collapsed section is the cause. A sewer line repair decision belongs to the owner, so we hand the manager the footage.

For the clog itself, drain cleaning with a cable or hydro jet clears the blockage, and a camera afterward tells you if it will come back. Repeat backups every few weeks point to a damaged line, not bad luck.
