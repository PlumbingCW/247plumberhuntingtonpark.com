When a storefront leak or backup is an emergency

The Pacific Boulevard shop corridor runs with a lot of tenants in a small area, and many buildings share walls, roofs and drain lines. That means a leak in one shop often shows up in another, and a restroom backup can reach a neighbor's floor. A burst supply line, sewage coming up through a floor drain or a flooded sales floor needs a plumber the same day, not next week.

Water that is clean and contained, like a slow drip under a sink, can wait for a scheduled visit. Sewage and active spraying cannot.

2

Property managers, landlords and tenants: who calls whom

Nearly three quarters of occupied homes in Huntington Park are rented, and many commercial buildings follow the same split: an owner, a manager and tenants who do not control the pipes. In a mixed-use building with shops below and apartments above, a single leak can cross all three. Decide before an emergency who has authority to approve a repair after hours.

We give the manager and the tenant the same plain description of the cause and the work. We do not claim insurance partnerships, and we recommend you photograph damage and contact your carrier yourself.

Commercial leak detection: what it can and cannot find

A shop that loses water at night or sees a steady bill increase needs a methodical search. We start with a meter test, then use acoustic listening equipment, thermal imaging and pressure testing to narrow the source before anyone cuts into a wall or slab.

Detection is not magic. Some leaks are hard to hear, and some are found only when the line is isolated and tested in sections. We tell you what the tools show and what they do not, and we do not open walls until the evidence points to a place. For larger or hidden leaks, see our page on leak detection.

6

Backflow testing: a planned job

Backflow preventers keep water in a building from flowing back into the public supply. Commercial buildings with irrigation, fire lines, boilers or certain restaurant equipment often have testable assemblies, such as a reduced pressure zone device or a double check valve. Many water suppliers require periodic testing.

Rules on who may test, how often and what forms apply come from the water supplier and the county or state. Ask the City Water & Sewer Division what your account needs, and confirm the tester's qualifications yourself before booking. If you are not sure whether your building has an assembly, a plumber can identify it on a walk-through.

Grease trap and interceptor cleaning

Restaurants, bakeries and food counters send fats, oils and grease down the drain, and an interceptor catches most of it before it hits the sewer. When the trap fills, grease passes through and solidifies downstream, and the kitchen drain slows and smells. A full trap should be pumped on a schedule rather than after a backup.

If a kitchen line backs up repeatedly after the trap is serviced, the line itself may need to be scoured with a hydro jetting nozzle. We recommend a camera look before assuming the trap is the whole problem.

9

Storm drain cleaning

Parking lots, loading areas and rooftop drains collect leaves, trash and silt, and a clogged catch basin sends water into doorways instead of the street. This is a planned maintenance job, best done before a storm season. Property managers should include roof drains and yard drains on a regular schedule and note which drains connect to the city storm system versus the sanitary sewer. If you are not sure, ask the city before flushing anything.
