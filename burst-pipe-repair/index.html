1

When a burst pipe is an emergency

It is an emergency whenever water is spraying, pouring through a ceiling, soaking a wall or running faster than you can mop. Shut off the main valve and call right away, because water that reaches drywall, insulation and electrical boxes does damage by the minute.

We answer after-hours and weekend calls, and same-day appointments are available when the day allows. A slow weep from a joint is less urgent than a spray, but it can fail suddenly, so do not leave it for later without a plan.

2

The first ten minutes after a pipe bursts

What you do before the plumber arrives changes how much needs to be replaced afterward.

  1. 1
    Shut off the main water valveTurn a round gate handle clockwise or a lever ball valve a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe. If it is stuck, do not force it with a wrench.
  2. 2
    Open a low faucetThis drains the pressure and the leftover water in the lines so the break stops flowing.
  3. 3
    Cut power if water is near electricitySwitch off the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Otherwise stay out and call 911.
  4. 4
    Turn off the water heaterSwitch off the gas or electric control so the tank does not heat while the lines are empty.
  5. 5
    Move belongings and soak up waterLift rugs, electronics and paper off the floor. Use towels, buckets or a wet vac.
  6. 6
    Photograph the damage, then callTake pictures for insurance, note where the water came from and ring the 24/7 line.
3

Until the plumber arrives

Once the water is off, these steps limit the mess.

  1. 1
    Leave the faucet openKeeping a low tap open helps drain the line, and closing it again can wait for the plumber.
  2. 2
    Clear the areaMove stored items away from the damaged pipe so we can work right away.
  3. 3
    Dry what you canFans and towels help, but do not run vacuums near outlets in standing water.
  4. 4
    Keep kids and pets awayWet floors and exposed pipe ends are slip and cut hazards.
  5. 5
    Write down what you sawNote when it started, where, and whether any work had recently been done there.

If you cannot find or close the main valve, tell us when you call and we will talk you through it. Replacement pipe and fittings are chosen to meet the California Plumbing Code (UPC based), not just to stop the spray.

4

Finding and closing the shutoff valve

Most homes have a main shutoff where the water line enters, often on a front or side wall, in a garage, or in a meter-side box. Learn where yours is before you need it. Test it once a year so it does not seize on the day it matters.

There is often a second shutoff at the street meter. That valve and the meter belong to the water provider, so for Huntington Park addresses ask the City of Huntington Park Water and Sewer Division before using or replacing anything on the utility side. For a leak on the buried line, see our water line repair page.

6

Cracked or broken copper pipe

Copper is common behind walls and under floors in older homes. A split often follows a bad solder joint, a pinhole worn by years of flow or a fitting damaged during other work. We cut back to clean, sound copper, then join new pipe with soldered or press-fit couplings, and pressure test before closing the wall.

If the leak is one of several signs of aging pipe, such as green corrosion, blue-green stains or repeat pinholes, we say so. A patch is honest and legitimate for one failure. It is not a promise about the rest of the line.

8

PVC, CPVC and broken drain pipe

Plastic supply pipe usually fails at fittings or after impact, and the repair is a clean cut and a new section with fittings. A drain pipe that cracks does not spray like a supply line, but it can flood a floor, soak a ceiling below or let sewer odors into the house.

Broken drain pipe repair means finding the crack, often with a camera, and replacing that stretch with matching pipe. For widespread problems in the main line, our sewer line repair page covers the next step.

9

Burst pipe or just a leak? How to tell

A burst pipe is a sudden loss of water with visible spray, a gushing sound or a rapidly growing wet area. A leak is slower, such as a drip at a joint, a damp patch or a meter that creeps. Both are worth repair, but they call for different speed.

Leaks you cannot see are a detection problem first. If there is no obvious wet spot, our leak detection page explains how we locate the source before opening a wall. Freezing is a rare cause in the Los Angeles basin, so we look instead at pressure, age, corrosion and damage from other trades.

10

What causes pipes to break here

Knowing the likely cause helps the repair last.

  • ✓
    High water pressurePressure above what fixtures are rated for strains joints. A pressure gauge on a hose bib shows your number, and a worn pressure regulator can be replaced.
  • ✓
    Age and corrosionOld copper and galvanized pipe thin from the inside, and failure can come with no warning.
  • ✓
    Damage from remodel workA nail, screw or drill bit can nick a pipe that fails weeks later.
  • ✓
    Water hammerQuick-closing valves send shock waves through lines. Arrestors or air chambers can calm it.
  • ✓
    Settling and movementShifting soil or a house that moves slightly can stress rigid pipe at joints.
11

Do not trust a temporary patch for long

Pipe repair clamps, epoxy putty and tape can stop a leak for a day, but they are not a repair. They are a bridge to the real fix. If you installed one yourself, tell the plumber, because it can hide corrosion that needs to be cut out. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Huntington Park building department if the work involves more than a simple section replacement.
