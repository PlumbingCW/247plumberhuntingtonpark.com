Tankless Water Heater in Huntington Park, CA

A tankless water heater in Huntington Park, CA heats water only when a tap opens, using a gas burner or electric elements and a heat exchanger instead of a stored tank. We install new units, repair ones that fault or leak, and descale the heat exchanger that hard water slowly clogs.

Hot water cutting out, turning lukewarm or throwing a code?

A shower that goes cold in the middle, a flow that trickles, or a display flashing a number are the common signs of a tankless problem. Most of them trace back to scale in the heat exchanger, a clogged inlet screen, a venting fault or a unit that was never sized for the load. That is what a tankless water heater service visit is built to sort out.

3

Installing a tankless unit

The usual surprise is the gas line. Many tankless gas models draw far more gas at full burn than a tank does, so the existing line, meter capacity and shutoff must be checked. Venting is the other big item, since most units need dedicated stainless or special plastic vent runs and a clear termination point.

Electric tankless models skip the vent but can need a very large circuit and panel capacity, which in older apartments and small homes is not always available. We verify the gas or electrical side first, then size the unit by how many fixtures run at once. A planning visit is part of our water heater installation work, and we also install isolation valves so the unit can be flushed later.

Common tankless repairs

These are the faults we see most on tankless heaters.

  • ✓
    Error codesCodes usually point to ignition, flame sensing, venting, flow or temperature sensors. We read the code, then test the part it names.
  • ✓
    Cold water sandwichA burst of cold between hot streaks often means a small flow that does not trigger the unit, or a recirculation setup issue.
  • ✓
    Low or fluctuating flowA clogged inlet filter, scaled exchanger or a partly closed isolation valve restricts water.
  • ✓
    LeaksLeaks at unions, a cracked heat exchanger or a failed relief valve need different fixes. A cracked exchanger may mean replacing the unit.
  • ✓
    Condensation and drain faultsHigh-efficiency models make acidic condensate and need a clear drain and a neutralizer cartridge that is checked.

If a tankless heater started to leak, shut the cold supply and power off, then call. Some leaks hide inside the cabinet.

5

Flush and descale: the upkeep that decides how long it lasts

The city's figures are 214 mg/L for groundwater and 235 mg/L for MWD blend water. Dividing by 17.1 gives about 12.5 and 13.7 grains per gallon, and the USGS calls anything over 180 mg/L very hard. For a tankless heater, that scale forms on the narrow heat exchanger passages, cuts flow, raises exhaust temperature and triggers codes.

Descaling circulates a food-safe acid solution through the exchanger using a small pump and two hoses connected to the isolation valves. We also clean the inlet screen and check the vent and condensate drain. How often depends on your tap and your usage, and the blend varies by season, so test your tap with a cheap hardness kit before you pick a schedule.

A softener can protect the exchanger

Descaling treats the symptom. A softener removes most of the calcium and magnesium before the water reaches the heater, so scale builds far more slowly. For a tankless unit in very hard water, the combination of a softener and an annual flush is the most reliable setup we know of.

The trade-off is cost and salt maintenance, which we explain on our water softener and filtration page. If you are on the fence, a hardness test shows how large the problem is.

9

Is tankless worth it for your home?

Tankless suits households that run out of hot water, want to free closet space, or have steady daily use. It can disappoint homes with a very tight gas supply, long pipe runs, or low-flow fixtures that never fire the burner. Ask for a load calculation before you buy, and ask for the maintenance plan in writing.
