1

When a leaking water heater is an emergency

A tank that drips from a pipe fitting is a repair. A tank that is pouring water from the body or seam is usually finished, and every hour of leaking soaks drywall, flooring and anything stored nearby. Garages and hall closets are common spots in Huntington Park homes and apartments, so the damage often reaches a neighbor or a shared wall.

Tell the dispatcher what you see: where the water is coming from, how fast, and whether the heater is gas or electric. That lets us bring the right valves, elements and fittings on the first trip. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and the emergency line covers nights and weekends.

3

What to do while you wait

These steps limit damage and speed up the visit.

  1. 1
    Shut off the cold supplyTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. If it will not move, use the main shutoff for the house.
  2. 2
    Cut power or gasFor electric units, switch off the heater breaker. For gas units, turn the dial to off or pilot. Do not do this if you smell gas, just leave.
  3. 3
    Protect the floorMove boxes and stored items, lay towels, and use a wet vacuum if you have one. Keep children and pets away from hot water.
  4. 4
    Note the labelTake a photo of the data plate showing brand, model, serial number and tank size, plus any error code.
  5. 5
    Clear the pathMake sure we can reach the heater, and tell us about stairs, a locked garage or an apartment shared utility closet.

If the heater sits in a rental, tell your landlord or property manager as well, since they often own the unit. California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires water heaters to be braced against earthquake motion, so we check straps while the tank is open.

4

Leaks: fitting, valve or tank?

Before we call a tank dead, we dry every joint with a rag and watch where the first bead of water forms. Water runs downhill, so a drip at the base can start at the top. We check the supply connections, the relief valve, the drain valve, the burner compartment or element covers, and the water line that runs from the heater into the house.

That last line matters. A pinhole in a buried or in-wall hot line can mimic a heater leak, and we confirm the source before quoting anything. If the loss is somewhere else, our leak detection methods, such as acoustic listening and thermal imaging, find it without opening walls at random.

5

No hot water, lukewarm water and pilot problems

Check these quickly before you call. They separate a ten-minute reset from a part replacement.

  • ✓
    Gas unit, pilot or burner darkLook at the dial and the viewing window. A pilot that will not stay lit often has a worn thermocouple, and one that lights but gives weak heat can mean a dirty burner.
  • ✓
    Electric unit, breaker and resetCheck the double-pole breaker, then the red reset button behind the upper panel. If it trips again, a failed element or thermostat is the usual reason.
  • ✓
    Water is warm but not hotOn electric heaters, one dead element gives half the hot water. On gas units, a thermostat set too low or a sediment layer can do the same.
  • ✓
    Error codes on the displayMany newer gas and hybrid units flash a code. Write it down with the brand and model, because it tells us which sensor or board to test.
  • ✓
    Hot water only at one tapThat points to a mixing valve or cartridge at the fixture, not the heater. We would look at the faucet or shower valve instead.

Never relight a pilot if you smell gas. The safety note below covers what to do.

6

Gas, electric and hybrid heat pump repairs differ

The same symptom has different fixes depending on how the heater makes heat, so here is how we approach each type.

Gas tank heaters

The parts that fail most are the thermocouple, the gas control valve, the burner assembly and the vent draft. We test for proper combustion and a clear flue before we leave, because a blocked or backdrafting vent is a safety problem, not a comfort problem.

Electric tank heaters

Here we use a meter to test each heating element and thermostat for continuity, and we check the wiring for scorching. Elements are inexpensive parts, but a tank full of thick scale can make them fail again soon, so a flush often goes with the repair.

Hybrid and heat pump models

These have a compressor, fan, condensate drain and electronic controls on top of a normal tank. Many faults are simple, such as a clogged air filter or condensate line, but some board and sensor repairs depend on the manufacturer. We say up front when a part is a warranty matter for the maker instead.

8

Flushing and anode rod service

Huntington Park tap water is very hard by the USGS scale. The city reports an average of 214 mg/L for local groundwater and 235 mg/L for Metropolitan Water District supply, roughly 12.5 and 13.7 grains per gallon. That scale settles on the tank floor, coats elements and burners, and shortens heater life, and the blend can shift by tap and season.

A yearly drain and flush clears loose sediment, and checking the anode rod tells us how much protection is left. A rod worn down to its steel core can be swapped for a fraction of the cost of a new tank, which is why we recommend it on heaters under about eight years old. If scale keeps returning, a softener or filter treats the cause.

9

Safety note for gas heaters

If you smell gas near the heater, do not touch switches, flames or phones in the room. Leave the home, call SoCalGas or 911 from a safe place, and call us afterward. We will not relight or repair a gas appliance until the leak is cleared.

10

When repair stops making sense

Repair is usually the better call when the tank is sound and the failed part is a thermostat, element, valve or thermocouple. Replace is usually better when the shell leaks, the tank is well past a decade, or the repair would cost close to half of a new unit. We give you both numbers, not a sales push.

Our full guide to that choice is on the water heater replacement page. If your family is outgrowing the tank, an on-demand model may be worth a look, which the tankless water heater page explains.
